CS Observation & Record-Keeping Guide

Quick-reference observation triggers for tracking Computational Thinking across Primary (3–6), Lower Elementary (6–9), and Upper Elementary (9–12) work cycles directly in Transparent Classroom.

CS Shelf Observation Focus

Observing Computational Thinking during CS Unplugged shelf work centers on watching how a child isolates problems, constructs logical sequences, and engages with built-in Controls of Error.

Because CS Unplugged shelf works function like traditional Montessori materials, status updates in Transparent Classroom translate directly from physical shelf behaviors—not written quizzes or adult-led verification.

Observation Triggers

  • Control of Error Engagement: Child checks mini-map keys, parity counts, or exit lines before declaring completion.
  • Independent Debugging: Child steps back to the last working command card when an obstacle is hit.
  • Peer Mentorship: Child guides younger peers or acts as "Control Key / Debugger" in pair work.

CT Competency Observational Indicators

Look for these specific behaviors when evaluating student interaction with CS Unplugged shelf baskets and floor mats:

1

Decomposition

Task Breakdown

Ability to segment complex grid routes or multi-step algorithms into distinct sub-tasks.

Practiced: Breaks a long Kidbot path into 2–3 manageable sub-sequences on the work rug.

Mastered: Defines reusable function modules (e.g., custom U-Turn cards) prior to laying code.

Needs More: Overwhelmed by multi-step challenges; requires guide to cover extra steps or shorten path.

2

Pattern Recognition & Abstraction

Efficiency & Rules

Spotting repeated command blocks and recognizing shared rules across different shelf works.

Practiced: Replaces consecutive directional cards with a `REPEAT 3x` card independently.

Mastered: Bridges Base-10 Stamp Game place-value concepts directly to Base-2 Binary Card flips.

Needs More: Lays duplicate single-step cards repeatedly without recognizing loop opportunities.

3

Algorithmic Execution

Order & Logic

Constructing linear, unambiguous instruction sequences to achieve predictable outcomes.

Practiced: Navigates sorting networks or floor grids accurately following laid command cards.

Mastered: Evaluates multiple valid paths and selects the shortest, most efficient route independently.

Needs More: Skips cards or executes steps out of sequence; benefits from using a tracking pointer.

4

Debugging & Control of Error

Self-Correction

Using built-in material error controls to systematically isolate and correct logic flaws.

Practiced: Pauses execution at an error, steps back to the last valid step, and replaces faulty card.

Mastered: Resolves complex bugs without adult intervention; assists peers in isolating logic flaws.

Needs More: Erases or clears entire work setup when one error occurs; bypasses answer keys.

Transparent Classroom Status Triggers

Mapping observable CS shelf work directly to Transparent Classroom progress stages:

View TC Lesson Import Details
Stage 1 I

Introduced

Group Presentation / Initial Demo

Child attended key lesson presentation or guide demonstration. Needs guide scaffolding or peer support during first shelf attempt.

Transparent Classroom Action: Tag `@child` during or immediately following small group presentation.
Stage 2 P

Practiced

Independent / Pair Shelf Work

Child selects material independently from shelf. Works through Level 1 & 2 cards and uses Control of Error keys to self-check.

TC Action: Snap rug photo + tag `@child #CS_LessonName [P]`.
Stage 3 M

Mastered

Fluency & Peer Leadership

Child completes advanced challenge cards effortlessly, optimizes code (loops/functions), debugs calmly, or presents work to a peer.

Transparent Classroom Action: Mark `[M]` and offer transition to digital bridge or next tier.
Stage 4 NM

Needs More

Targeted Re-Presentation

Child bypasses solution keys, displays spatial directionality confusion, or abandons activity when bugs occur.

Guide Action: Plan 1-on-1 re-presentation or isolate prerequisite material.

Sensorial & Practical Life Connections

Directionality & Grid Navigation
Walks physical floor path when guided with 1-step arrow cards during presentation. Lays 3–4 card sequences (Forward, Turn Right) to move "Kidbot" token from Start to Target on rug. Lays 5+ card routes around obstacles; acts as "Robot" following peer-generated code. Confuses left/right arrow orientation repeatedly on rug grid.
Control of Error & Debugging
Notices when Kidbot steps off grid or hits an obstacle during guide-led demo. Flips card over to verify against mini-map Control Key; replaces wrong arrow card. Isolates exact card causing error ("Bug"), fixes it independently, and re-tests route. Ignores Control Key on back of card; claims route is correct when off-target.
Binary State & Dot Matching
Matches 1-to-1 dot cards (1, 2, 4 dots) to physical counters or card faces. Counts binary dots on/off (visible/hidden) to represent numbers 1 through 7. Flips cards independently to show given numbers up to 15 without 1-by-1 counting. Struggles with visible vs. hidden state concept (card showing dots vs. blank side).
Digital Bridge (ScratchJr)
Explores ScratchJr app interface; taps motion blocks to observe sprite movements. Snaps 3–4 icon blocks together (Green Flag + Move Right + Jump) to execute sequence. Creates mini-story with 2 sprites interacting using message envelope triggers. Taps screen randomly without connecting block icons to sprite action.
6–9

Lower Elementary Plane (Ages 6–9) Focus Area

Efficiency, Loop Structures, 5-Bit Binary Encoding, Sorting Networks & Scratch

Reasoning Mind & Base-10 Math Alignment
CS Competency Domain Introduced (I) Practiced (P) Mastered (M) Needs More (NM)
Loops & Optimization
Replaces repeated forward cards with a `REPEAT 3x` card with initial guide prompting. Identifies repeating patterns in 5x5 grid paths and incorporates loop cards independently. Writes shortest possible algorithm using loops and conditional logic (`IF OBSTACLE`). Lays 10+ duplicate single-step cards; resists using loop cards even when guided.
5-Bit Binary Encoding
Uses 5-bit key table to encode single initials into binary bead bracelets. Encodes 3-letter words (e.g., "CAT") into 5-bit binary code without adult guidance. Decodes secret binary messages accurately and explains Base-2 exponent math (1,2,4,8,16). Mixes up 0/1 states; unable to track 5-bit groupings across letters.
Sorting Networks
Walks chalk sorting network path, comparing number cards at nodes with partner. Executes correct Left (smaller) vs. Right (larger) path decisions at comparison nodes. Facilitates outdoor sorting network group, explains parallel processing, and spots routing bugs. Ignores node comparison rules; walks randomly across chalk network lines.
Digital Bridge (Scratch)
Transfers unplugged grid steps into Scratch block sequences (When Clicked, Move). Builds functional Scratch scripts using Repeat loops and Forever blocks for animation. Develops Eco-Sorter capstone game in Scratch with variables and collision detection. Struggles to map physical arrow cards to Scratch visual blocks.
9–12

Upper Elementary Plane (Ages 9–12) Focus Area

Cryptography, Error Parity, Modulo Math, Custom Functions & Python/Alice Bridge

Abstract Reasoning & Social Mind Alignment
CS Competency Domain Introduced (I) Practiced (P) Mastered (M) Needs More (NM)
Functions & Sub-Routines
Defines sub-routine maneuvers (e.g., "U-Turn") onto dry-erase CUSTOM ACTION function cards. Decomposes 5x5 grid challenges into reusable sub-functions and nested loops. Designs original challenge cards with custom function keys for younger peers. Struggles to break large challenges down; relies on monolithic single-step lists.
Parity Magic & Checksums
Sets up 5x5 grid of binary cards and adds parity row/column checksum cards with guide help. Detects flipped card ("magic trick") independently by calculating row/column bit parity. Explains RAID storage parity and checksum transmission reliability in modern computer networks. Fails to track even/odd card count across rows; relies on visual guessing.
Cryptography & Modulo Math
Uses Caesar cipher wheels to shift text messages by fixed numeric keys (+3 offset). Encodes and decodes multi-word secret messages using Modulo-26 remainder arithmetic. Constructs public/private key demonstration model and mentors younger peers on cryptographic security. Confused by remainder arithmetic (Modulo); struggles to wrap alphabet letters Z back to A.
Digital Text Bridge (Alice / Python)
Compares visual Scratch blocks side-by-side with text pseudocode translation tables. Builds interactive Python/Alice scripts utilizing conditional logic, variables, and arrays. Completes Plant Care Tracker capstone project with hardware/sensor logic simulation. Intimidated by text syntax; unable to relate text variables back to unplugged concepts.

Fast Transparent Classroom Observation Workflow

1
Initial Presentation (`I`) Log Introduced when a child attends a small group presentation or demonstration of a new CS shelf basket.
2
Independent Work Cycle Practice (`P`) Observe the child working independently or in pairs with the shelf material. Check whether they use the back-of-card Control Key to self-correct. Update status to Practiced.
3
Photo & Mastery Tagging (`M`) Snap a photo of the completed rug or bracelet setup. Tag @child #CS_LessonName and select Mastered when the child demonstrates independent debugging and explains the logic to a peer.
4
Targeted Support (`NM`) Mark Needs More if a child repeatedly skips self-correction or displays confusion on core directional/binary concepts to flag a 1-on-1 re-presentation.

Guide Prompting Reference

Socratic Prompts to Encourage Self-Correction

  • When a robot strays off path: "Where did your token move somewhere unexpected? Can you trace your arrow cards step-by-step against the Control Key to locate the bug?"
  • When code is repetitive: "Do you notice a repeating sequence in your arrow cards? How could you use a `REPEAT` loop card to make your code shorter?"
  • Checking binary card values: "Does your dot count match the total on our key card? Is this card flipped 'On' (1) or 'Off' (0)?"
Non-Disruptive Guide Intervention

Observing Built-in Control of Error Across CS Materials

Observe whether students actively use material controls of error before seeking guide assistance:

Grid & Spatial Works

Mini-map solution keys on card backs or immediate spatial feedback when landing on a grid obstacle.

Binary & Data Works

Control cards with visual dot sums, parity odd/even row counts, or decoded letter reference tables.

Network Works

Final exit line standing in ascending numerical order provides visual proof of execution accuracy.
